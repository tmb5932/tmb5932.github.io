fixed-wing-drone / build.log

My roommate and I are building an autonomous fixed-wing RC plane from scratch. He's a mechanical engineer who designed and built the airframe and control surfaces, picked the motors and servos, and mounted everything in the plane. I designed the flight controller, which is all of the electronics and all of the software. It sits between a standard RC receiver and the plane's servos and ESCs. In manual mode it passes the pilot's commands straight through, and when the pilot flips a switch it takes over, holding attitude with PID loops and steering toward GPS waypoints.

hardware: ESP32-S3, custom carrier PCB (flown) and custom v3 PCB (ordered), ICM-20948 IMU, GPS, MS4525DO pitot airspeed sensor

// what the flight controller does

firmware/

  • Cascaded control. A 10 Hz heading loop turns the next GPS waypoint into a bank angle, and 100 Hz roll and pitch loops hold it. An airspeed PID sets throttle, and the speed target rises in turns to keep the same margin above stall.
  • The control loop gets one ESP32-S3 core to itself on a fixed 100 Hz schedule, while the sensor and navigation tasks share the other, so a slow sensor read can't delay a control cycle.
  • The pilot is always in charge. Flipping to manual always takes back control. RC gaps under 1 s hold the last command, and longer ones send the plane home and then into a spiral descent with the motor off.
  • Autonomous mode can't engage until the switch has been seen in manual since boot, stays off without a GPS fix, and hands control back after five missed attitude reads.
  • Two host-side SITL simulators compile the exact same PID and GPS-math source the firmware runs, and 80 regression tests gate every gain change.
  • A setup mode (hold BOOT at power-on) starts a WiFi captive portal for calibrating servos and ESCs, tuning PID gains, and planning GPS missions.

hardware/revisions/

breadboard

bench prototype
retired

ESP32-S3 devkit and standalone sensor modules on a breadboard. Proved out the sensor drivers, RC pulse capture, PWM outputs, and the first control loop before moving to a real board.

v1 carrier board

first PCB
retired

My first PCB: a carrier board for the ESP32-S3 devkit with nearly the same layout v2 ended up with, moving the breadboard wiring onto real connectors for the servos, ESCs, receiver, and sensors.

Bringing it up turned up a few mistakes. Some connectors had TX and RX swapped, so I bridged them with jumper wires. The airspeed sensor had the wrong connector and only got 3.3V instead of the 5V it needs. Some RC inputs landed on pins that aren't usable on my devkit, and a few silkscreen labels were misprinted. v2 fixed all of it.

v2 carrier board

the board that has flown
flown

Carrier board for the ESP32-S3 devkit. Routes the receiver, 6 servo and 2 ESC outputs, and the off-board IMU, GPS, and pitot airspeed sensor to proper connectors.

Splits power into isolated servo and logic rails, so a stalling servo can't brown out the flight controller.

v3 custom board

ordered, not yet flown
ordered

Fully custom board. Primary and backup IMUs onboard over SPI from different manufacturers (ICM-42688-P and BMI088), a DPS368 barometer for altitude, and microSD logging.

Heading moves to an external compass mounted away from the motor and power wiring, and the RC input becomes a serial line for a digital receiver.

2026-10-10 manual Flew, but with frequent control dropouts. Our receiver drops out briefly at range, and the 200 ms link-loss threshold was treating those gaps as a lost link. Raised it to 1 s.
